**Title:** Live Worm Farm 🪱 Red wigglers composting behind glass, around the clock

**Description:**

A glass-sided worm bin, live. Around a hundred red wigglers are turning kitchen scraps into compost as you watch.
Nothing is scripted and nothing repeats. Every worm decides for itself where to crawl, when to eat and when to
rest.

**What you're watching**

- The top layer is shredded cardboard and newspaper bedding. Below it is dark compost and soil.
- Every few hours the keeper drops in scraps: banana peel, apple core, lettuce, carrot peelings, coffee grounds,
  eggshell. They brown, soften and grow mould. As they rot, their smell spreads and the worms find them and gather
  on them in knots to feed.
- Worms that press against the glass leave burrows that slowly fill back in, and dark castings (worm compost)
  build up where they've been.
- Adults lay small lemon-shaped cocoons that hatch into pale young worms, which grow into adults over the day.
- When the top dries out, the bin gets a misting and the glass fogs with condensation that clears over the
  next minutes.
- All the sound is the bin itself: bedding crackling as worms crawl, soft feeding sounds, scraps landing, the
  spray bottle.

**Good to know**

- It's a computer simulation, rendered in real time, of what a worm farm looks like. It's tuned to look
  believable over a whole day, not to model red wigglers precisely.
- No real animals are involved.
